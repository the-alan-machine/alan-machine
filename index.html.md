## How to read this book

This book is about a machine that does not exist. We call it Alan, and we use it to ask a question
physics takes very seriously: can entropy be reversed, and if it could, what would happen to time?

You do not need a physics degree to read it. The chapters are written for anyone who is curious.
When the math helps, it shows up in boxes marked *Going deeper*, which you can open or skip. The
story works either way.

Some chapters have a companion paper, written for specialists, with the full argument. You will
find the papers near the end of the book, and each chapter tells you when one exists.

Each part ends with an *interlude*: a short pause for a question that physics can inform but not
answer, such as whether time really flows or whether a machine can think. After the main text,
*Building Alan* looks at real technology, from today's chips to quantum computers, and asks how far
each one is from the limits the chapters describe. Those pages are updated every year.

Along the way, we keep four kinds of statement apart. Unmarked claims are **established** physics,
with references you can check. Boxes marked **Extrapolation** follow from established physics, but
only if their stated assumptions hold. Boxes marked **Speculation** hold ideas that are conceivable
but that no evidence supports yet. Boxes marked **Philosophy** hold questions that physics cannot
settle, with the best arguments on each side. A thought experiment is only useful if you always know
which of the four you are reading.

Every technical term links to its plain-language definition in the Concepts appendix at the end.
